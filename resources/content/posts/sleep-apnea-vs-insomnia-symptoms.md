## Why Can Sleep Apnea and Insomnia Feel So Similar?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Common Sleep Disorders: Symptoms, Causes & Health Effects](/health-library/common-sleep-disorders-symptoms-health-effects/).

If you regularly wake up tired, struggle to stay asleep, or find yourself exhausted during the day, it can be difficult to know what is actually causing the problem.

Insomnia and [sleep apnea](/conditions/sleep-apnea/) are different sleep disorders, but their symptoms can overlap. Both can leave you feeling unrefreshed, fatigued, and less able to concentrate during the day.

The difference is often found in what is happening during sleep.

## What Is Insomnia?

Insomnia is a sleep disorder involving persistent difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or getting good-quality sleep despite having an opportunity to sleep.

Someone with insomnia may lie awake for a long time before falling asleep, wake repeatedly during the night, or wake earlier than intended and struggle to return to sleep.

Stress, anxiety, medications, medical conditions, irregular schedules, and other factors can contribute to insomnia.

## Typical Clues

People with insomnia often notice the problem themselves because they are aware of being awake or unable to sleep.

They may also experience daytime fatigue, irritability, difficulty concentrating, or concerns about getting enough sleep.

## What Is Sleep Apnea?

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) occurs when the upper airway repeatedly becomes narrowed or blocked during sleep, disrupting normal breathing.

A person may not remember these breathing interruptions. Instead, a bed partner may notice loud snoring, choking, gasping, or pauses in breathing.

Sleep apnea can also cause fragmented sleep and excessive daytime sleepiness.

## Common Warning Signs

Look for clues such as:

- Loud, frequent snoring

- Gasping or choking during sleep

- Breathing pauses noticed by another person

- Morning headaches

- Dry mouth after waking

- Excessive daytime sleepiness

- Difficulty concentrating

Not everyone with sleep apnea snores, so the absence of snoring does not completely rule it out.

## Where the Symptoms Overlap

Both conditions can cause:

- Poor-quality sleep

- Daytime tiredness

- Difficulty concentrating

- Irritability or mood changes

- Frequent nighttime awakenings

This overlap is one reason people may assume they have insomnia when an underlying sleep-related breathing disorder is actually contributing to their symptoms.

## The Clues That Point Toward Sleep Apnea

One of the most useful distinctions is how the sleep problem presents.

If you are awake for long periods worrying about sleep or unable to fall asleep despite feeling tired, insomnia may be contributing.

If someone tells you that you snore loudly, stop breathing, gasp, or choke during sleep, sleep apnea becomes more important to consider.

Other clues include persistent morning headaches, dry mouth, or significant daytime sleepiness despite apparently sleeping for enough hours.

## You Can Have Both

Insomnia and sleep apnea are not mutually exclusive.

Some people can have difficulty falling or staying asleep while also experiencing repeated breathing disturbances during sleep. When symptoms persist, it is important to avoid assuming there is only one explanation.

## How Are They Diagnosed?

Diagnosis begins with a detailed medical and sleep history.

Your doctor may ask about your sleep schedule, nighttime symptoms, daytime alertness, medications, lifestyle, and other medical conditions.

If sleep apnea is suspected, a sleep study may be recommended. Depending on your circumstances, this may involve a home sleep apnea test or an overnight laboratory study.

Insomnia is usually assessed through your sleep history and symptoms. A sleep diary or other assessment may sometimes be useful.

## When Should You Seek Help?

Consider speaking with a healthcare professional if sleep problems are persistent, affect your daily functioning, or leave you excessively sleepy during the day.

If you regularly struggle to stay awake while driving or performing potentially dangerous activities, take the situation seriously. Avoid driving when you are too sleepy and seek medical advice.

## Final Thoughts

Insomnia and sleep apnea can look similar from the outside because both can leave you tired, irritable, and unable to concentrate. The important difference is what is causing the disrupted sleep. Difficulty falling or staying asleep may suggest insomnia, while snoring, gasping, breathing pauses, and significant daytime sleepiness can point toward sleep apnea. If your sleep problems continue, a medical evaluation can help identify the underlying cause and guide appropriate treatment.
