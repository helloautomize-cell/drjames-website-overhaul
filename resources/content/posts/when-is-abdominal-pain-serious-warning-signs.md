## When Should You Worry About Abdominal Pain?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Abdominal Pain Causes & Gastritis: Symptoms and Treatment](/health-library/abdominal-pain-causes-gastritis-treatment/).

:::emergency

[Abdominal pain](/conditions/digestive-problems/) is extremely common and can have many causes. Indigestion, gas, constipation, or a mild infection may cause temporary discomfort. However, severe, sudden, persistent, or worsening abdominal pain can sometimes indicate a condition requiring urgent medical attention.

:::

The intensity of pain is important, but it is not the only factor. Where the pain occurs, how quickly it started, how it changes, and which other symptoms appear can all help your doctor determine what may be happening.

## Warning Signs You Should Not Ignore

Certain symptoms alongside abdominal pain deserve prompt medical attention.

## Severe or Rapidly Worsening Pain

Sudden, severe abdominal pain or pain that becomes progressively worse should not simply be treated at home.

Conditions such as appendicitis, gallbladder disease, bowel obstruction, pancreatitis, and other problems can cause significant abdominal pain and may require urgent assessment.

## Vomiting Blood or Passing Black Stools

Blood in vomit or black, tar-like stools can indicate bleeding somewhere in the digestive tract.

These symptoms require urgent medical evaluation, particularly when accompanied by weakness, dizziness, or fainting.

## Persistent Vomiting

Repeated vomiting can lead to dehydration and may occur with several abdominal conditions.

If you cannot keep fluids down, have severe abdominal pain, or develop weakness or dizziness, seek medical attention promptly.

## Fever With Abdominal Pain

A fever combined with abdominal pain can occur with infections or inflammation inside the abdomen.

The significance depends on the location and severity of the pain and the other symptoms present.

## Pay Attention to Where the Pain Occurs

The location of abdominal pain can provide useful clues.

Lower-right abdominal pain may occur with appendicitis, although other conditions can cause similar symptoms.

Upper-right abdominal pain may be associated with gallbladder or liver problems.

Upper abdominal pain can occur with conditions involving the stomach, pancreas, gallbladder, or other organs.

Pain around the side or back may sometimes be related to the kidneys or urinary tract.

However, location alone cannot establish a diagnosis. Several conditions can cause pain in the same area.

## Other Symptoms That Need Attention

Abdominal pain should be taken more seriously when it occurs with:

- Fainting or severe dizziness

- Significant abdominal swelling

- Blood in vomit or stool

- Persistent vomiting

- High fever or feeling severely unwell

- Yellowing of the skin or eyes

- Difficulty breathing or chest pain

- Unexplained weight loss

- Severe weakness

- New or severe pain during pregnancy

These symptoms can have different causes, but they warrant appropriate medical assessment.

## What About Mild but Persistent Pain?

Not all serious problems cause dramatic pain.

If abdominal discomfort continues for days, repeatedly returns, or gradually becomes worse, it is worth speaking with a doctor even if the pain is not severe.

Persistent symptoms can occur with conditions such as gastritis, ulcers, reflux, gallbladder disease, bowel disorders, or other medical problems.

Keeping track of when the pain occurs, what you eat, bowel movements, medications, and associated symptoms can help during a medical consultation.

## Avoid Masking the Problem

Taking repeated doses of pain medication without understanding the cause may delay appropriate evaluation. Some medicines, particularly certain NSAID painkillers, can also irritate the stomach or cause other complications in susceptible people.

If you need frequent medication for unexplained abdominal pain, discuss the problem with a healthcare professional.

## How Is Serious Abdominal Pain Evaluated?

A doctor may ask about the onset, location, severity, duration, and pattern of your pain.

A physical examination may be followed by blood tests, urine testing, ultrasound, CT, endoscopy, or other investigations when appropriate.

The correct evaluation depends on your symptoms and suspected cause.

## Final Thoughts

Most abdominal pain is not an emergency, but certain patterns and accompanying symptoms can signal a more serious problem. Severe or worsening pain, digestive bleeding, persistent vomiting, fainting, significant swelling, or other concerning symptoms should prompt medical attention rather than waiting for the problem to resolve. Even when pain is mild, recurring or persistent symptoms deserve evaluation. Understanding the warning signs can help you know when abdominal pain needs more than home care.
