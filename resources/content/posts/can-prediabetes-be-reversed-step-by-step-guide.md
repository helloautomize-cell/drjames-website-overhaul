A [prediabetes](/conditions/diabetes-and-prediabetes/) result can feel like a verdict, but it is closer to an early notice. Many people see their blood sugar return to the normal range after changes to activity, food, weight and sleep. The harder part is keeping those changes going long enough for the improvement to hold. Below is what tends to work, in the order most patients find easiest to start.

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Diabetes & Prediabetes: Early Warning Signs You Shouldn’t Ignore](/health-library/diabetes-and-prediabetes-early-warning-signs-you-shouldnt-ignore/).

## Step 1: Confirm Your Result and Note Your Numbers

Start by asking your doctor which test was done (HbA1c, fasting glucose or an oral glucose tolerance test) and what the value was. Write the number and the date down. That single figure becomes your baseline, and it is the only way to tell later whether your efforts are making a difference. A doctor may repeat the test before deciding what comes next, because one reading does not always reflect the full picture.

## Step 2: Start Moving

Muscles take up glucose more readily when they are active, and this effect happens even before any weight comes off. The usual target is at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, such as brisk walking. If you have been inactive for a while, begin with 10 to 15 minutes a day and add time gradually. A short walk after a meal can also soften the blood sugar rise that follows eating. If you have heart disease, joint problems or another condition, check with your doctor before starting.

## Step 3: Adjust What Is on Your Plate

Rice, bread and traditional sweets do not need to disappear. In practice, portion size and frequency make the biggest difference. Sugary drinks are often the easiest place to start, since they add a lot of sugar without much satisfaction. Some practical adjustments:

- Replace sweetened tea, soft drinks and juice with water or unsweetened drinks

- Build the plate around vegetables or salad, with rice or bread taking up less space

- Choose whole grains and pulses more often than white rice or refined flour

- Add a source of protein to each meal

- Treat desserts as occasional rather than daily

A plate of machboos can still fit into this pattern if the rice portion is smaller and the vegetables and protein are generous.

## Step 4: Work on Excess Weight, Gradually

For people carrying extra weight, a modest loss can noticeably improve how the body responds to insulin. Diabetes prevention studies have commonly used a goal of around 5 to 7 percent of body weight. Slow, steady loss tends to stick better than a strict diet that is hard to live with. A registered dietitian can help you shape a plan around your actual routine.

## Step 5: Look After Your Sleep and Stress

Short or broken sleep increases appetite and tends to reduce daily movement, and both push blood sugar in the wrong direction. Long periods of stress can affect it too. If you snore heavily, wake up unrefreshed or notice pauses in breathing during sleep, raise it with your doctor. Sleep apnoea is linked to insulin resistance, and it is worth investigating if it fits.

## Step 6: Keep Testing and Following Up

Your doctor will advise how often you need a repeat test. For people with prediabetes, this is often once a year. A better result is worth celebrating, but habits tend to slip quietly over time. If they do, blood sugar can climb again, so the follow-up is part of the treatment.

## Should Medication Be Considered?

For some people at higher risk, doctors may discuss medication alongside lifestyle changes. Whether it makes sense depends on age, weight, other health conditions and test results. It is a decision to make with your doctor, not one to take from an online article. For most people, lifestyle changes remain the foundation.

## When Should You See a Doctor?

:::emergency

[Book an appointment](/contact/) soon if you notice increased thirst, passing urine more often than usual, unexplained weight loss, blurred vision or wounds that are slow to heal. Seek urgent care if very high blood sugar is accompanied by vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, difficulty breathing or severe abdominal pain.

:::

## Final Thoughts

You do not need to overhaul your life in a week. Choose two or three changes you can realistically keep for months, then add to them as they become routine.
