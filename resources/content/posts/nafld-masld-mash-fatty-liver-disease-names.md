## Why Has Fatty Liver Disease Been Renamed?

If you have recently read about [fatty liver disease](/health-library/fatty-liver-disease-causes-symptoms-and-how-to-reverse-it/), you may have noticed several different terms: NAFLD, MASLD, and MASH.

These names can be confusing, but they reflect an important change in how doctors describe [fatty liver disease](/conditions/fatty-liver/).

The newer terminology was introduced to better describe the underlying metabolic factors associated with many cases of fatty liver disease and to move away from terminology based mainly on what the disease is not caused by.

The terms are related, but they are not interchangeable.

## What Did NAFLD Mean?

NAFLD stood for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

It described a condition in which excess fat accumulates in the liver without another clear cause, particularly significant alcohol-related liver disease.

NAFLD could range from relatively mild fat accumulation to more serious liver inflammation and damage.

Although the term was widely used, it had limitations. The word “non-alcoholic” described the absence of one cause rather than explaining the metabolic factors often associated with the condition.

## What Does MASLD Mean?

MASLD stands for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease.

“Steatotic liver disease” simply means that there is excess fat in the liver.

MASLD is associated with metabolic risk factors such as overweight or obesity, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, abnormal cholesterol or triglyceride levels, and other features of metabolic dysfunction.

The diagnosis is not based simply on having liver fat. A healthcare professional considers the overall clinical picture and excludes other relevant causes when appropriate.

## MASLD Is More Than a Liver Problem

The condition is closely connected with metabolic health. This means that managing blood glucose, blood pressure, weight, cholesterol, and other risk factors can be an important part of caring for someone with MASLD.

## What Does MASH Mean?

MASH stands for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis.

It describes a form of MASLD in which there is liver inflammation and liver cell injury associated with fat accumulation.

MASH is more concerning than simple liver fat because ongoing inflammation and injury can contribute to liver scarring, known as fibrosis.

Over time, advanced fibrosis can progress to cirrhosis in some people.

## Does Fatty Liver Always Cause Symptoms?

No. Many people with fatty liver disease have few or no noticeable symptoms, particularly in earlier stages.

Some people may experience tiredness or vague discomfort in the upper-right abdomen, but these symptoms are not specific to fatty liver disease.

This is why liver problems may sometimes be discovered during routine blood tests or imaging performed for another reason.

## How Is Fatty Liver Disease Evaluated?

Your doctor may consider medical history, blood tests, metabolic risk factors, and imaging. Additional tests may be used to assess whether liver fibrosis is present and how advanced it may be.

Not everyone with fatty liver has the same level of risk, so evaluation should be individualized.

## When to See a Doctor

:::emergency

See a doctor if you have been told you have fatty liver, if your liver tests are abnormal, or if you have diabetes, obesity or high cholesterol. Seek urgent care for yellowing of the skin or eyes, vomiting blood, black stools or sudden confusion.

:::

## Final Thoughts

The shift from NAFLD to MASLD and MASH can seem confusing, but the new terminology provides a clearer way to describe fatty liver disease and its relationship with metabolic health. If you have been told that you have fatty liver, understanding which term applies to your condition can help you have a more informed discussion with your doctor about liver health, risk factors, and appropriate follow-up.
