## Who Should Manage Your Kidney Health?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Kidney Health 101: Warning Signs You Shouldn’t Overlook](/health-library/kidney-health-101-warning-signs-you-shouldnt-overlook/).

If you have an abnormal kidney test, protein in your urine, or a diagnosis of kidney disease, you may wonder whether you should see a primary care doctor or a nephrologist.

There is no single answer for everyone. The right doctor depends on your kidney function, symptoms, underlying health conditions, and how complex your kidney problem is.

Many kidney health concerns can initially be evaluated by a primary care doctor. A nephrologist may become involved when a kidney condition is persistent, complicated, worsening, or requires specialist assessment.

## What Can a Primary Care Doctor Do?

Primary care doctors are often the first point of contact for kidney-related concerns. They can assess your overall health and identify factors that may affect your kidneys.

They may:

- Check blood pressure and blood glucose

- Order blood and urine tests

- Assess kidney function

- Identify possible risk factors for kidney disease

- Review medicines and supplements

- Monitor stable [kidney problems](/conditions/kidney-problems/)

- Manage conditions such as diabetes and high blood pressure

This broader approach is important because diabetes and high blood pressure are major conditions associated with kidney damage.

## When Primary Care May Be Enough

If your kidney function is stable and the underlying problem is straightforward, your primary care doctor may be able to monitor you with regular blood and urine testing.

They can also refer you to a nephrologist if your results or symptoms suggest that specialist evaluation would be beneficial.

## What Does a Nephrologist Do?

A nephrologist is a doctor who specializes in kidney diseases and kidney function.

A nephrologist may evaluate conditions such as chronic kidney disease, significant protein in the urine, persistent abnormalities in kidney function, electrolyte disturbances, and certain difficult-to-control blood pressure problems.

The specialist may review trends in your kidney tests, investigate possible causes, and recommend treatment or monitoring based on your individual situation.

## When Should You See a Nephrologist?

Specialist evaluation may be appropriate when:

- Kidney function continues to decline

- Protein in the urine is persistent or significant

- The cause of kidney abnormalities is unclear

- Kidney problems are complicated or difficult to manage

- There are significant electrolyte abnormalities

- Blood pressure remains difficult to control despite treatment

- Your primary care doctor believes specialist input is needed

A referral does not automatically mean you have severe kidney disease. Sometimes the purpose is simply to understand an abnormal result and determine the best way to protect kidney function.

## Can Both Doctors Work Together?

Absolutely. In many cases, primary care and nephrology provide complementary care.

The nephrologist can focus on the kidney-specific problem, while your primary care doctor continues to manage your overall health, including diabetes, blood pressure, cholesterol, medications, and other conditions.

This coordinated approach can be especially useful when several health conditions affect kidney health.

## Don't Wait for Symptoms

Kidney disease can sometimes develop without obvious symptoms. If you have diabetes, high blood pressure, or abnormal kidney test results, follow your doctor's recommendations for kidney monitoring even if you feel well.

## Final Thoughts

Your primary care doctor is often the right starting point for evaluating kidney health. If testing reveals a persistent, complex, or worsening kidney problem, a nephrologist can provide specialized assessment and management. Rather than choosing one doctor over the other, think of kidney care as a coordinated process designed to identify problems early and protect kidney function over time.
