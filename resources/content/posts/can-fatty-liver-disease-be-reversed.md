## Can Fatty Liver Disease Be Reversed?

In many people, [fatty liver disease](/health-library/fatty-liver-disease-causes-symptoms-and-how-to-reverse-it/) can improve when the factors contributing to excess liver fat are addressed. The extent of improvement depends on the underlying cause and whether liver inflammation or scarring is already present.

Today, fatty liver associated with metabolic dysfunction is commonly called MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease). It is often linked with conditions such as excess weight, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, and abnormal cholesterol or triglyceride levels.

The good news is that improving metabolic health can also support liver health.

## Step 1: Understand Your Liver Health

Before deciding what you need to do, find out how your liver is actually doing.

Your doctor may review liver blood tests, medical history, metabolic risk factors, and imaging. If appropriate, additional assessment may be used to determine whether liver fibrosis, or scarring, is present.

Fat accumulation alone does not tell the whole story. Understanding whether inflammation or fibrosis is present helps determine your individual risk and follow-up needs.

## Step 2: Work Toward a Healthy Weight

If you are overweight or have obesity, gradual and sustainable weight loss can help reduce liver fat and improve metabolic health.

Avoid extreme diets or rapid weight-loss programs promoted as “liver detoxes.” The goal is a realistic eating and activity pattern that you can maintain over time.

Your doctor or dietitian can help develop an approach appropriate for your health and nutritional needs.

## Step 3: Improve Your Everyday Diet

There is no single “fatty liver diet” that works for everyone.

A generally heart-healthy eating pattern can help support both metabolic and liver health. Focus on nutritious foods and appropriate portions while limiting foods that are high in added sugars, excess calories, and unhealthy fats.

Replacing highly processed foods with vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, and other nutrient-rich foods can be a practical starting point.

## Step 4: Stay Physically Active

Regular physical activity can benefit liver and metabolic health, even when significant weight loss does not occur.

Choose activities that are realistic for your fitness level, such as walking, cycling, swimming, or other forms of exercise you can perform consistently.

If you have not exercised regularly or have other medical conditions, speak with your healthcare professional about an appropriate activity plan.

## Step 5: Control Diabetes, Cholesterol, and Blood Pressure

[Fatty liver disease](/conditions/fatty-liver/) is closely connected with metabolic health.

If you have diabetes, high cholesterol, or high blood pressure, managing these conditions is an important part of protecting your liver and overall health.

Take prescribed medicines as directed and attend recommended follow-up appointments.

## Be Careful With Supplements

Do not assume that herbal products or “liver detox” supplements will reverse fatty liver. Some supplements can cause liver injury or interact with medicines.

Always discuss supplements with your healthcare professional before taking them.

## Step 6: Avoid Excess Alcohol

Alcohol can contribute to liver injury and may complicate the assessment of fatty liver disease. If you have liver disease, discuss alcohol use with your doctor so you can understand what is appropriate for your situation.

## When to See a Doctor

:::emergency

See a doctor if you have fatty liver and are not sure how advanced it is, if your tests are not improving, or if you have diabetes or high cholesterol. Seek urgent care for yellow skin or eyes, vomiting blood or black stools.

:::

## Final Thoughts

Fatty liver disease is not something you should simply ignore, but it also does not mean that liver damage is inevitable. Improving metabolic health through sustainable lifestyle changes and appropriate medical care can help protect the liver. If you have been diagnosed with fatty liver, speak with your doctor about your individual risk, whether fibrosis needs assessment, and the most appropriate plan for long-term liver health.
