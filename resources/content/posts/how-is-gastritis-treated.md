## How Is Gastritis Treated?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Abdominal Pain Causes & Gastritis: Symptoms and Treatment](/health-library/abdominal-pain-causes-gastritis-treatment/).

Gastritis is inflammation of the stomach lining, and treatment depends on the underlying cause. Simply treating stomach pain may provide temporary relief without addressing the reason the inflammation developed.

Gastritis can be associated with Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection, certain medications, alcohol, or other medical conditions. Some people have symptoms such as upper [abdominal pain](/conditions/digestive-problems/), nausea, bloating, or indigestion, while others may have few noticeable symptoms.

The first step is finding out what may be causing the problem.

## Step 1: Identify the Cause

Your doctor may ask about your symptoms, medications, medical history, alcohol use, and previous digestive problems.

One important question is whether you regularly use nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen or naproxen. These medicines can irritate the stomach lining in some people.

Your doctor may also consider whether H. pylori infection could be contributing to your symptoms.

## H. Pylori Testing

H. pylori can affect the stomach lining and is an important cause of gastritis and peptic ulcer disease.

Depending on your situation, testing may involve a breath test, stool test, blood test, or other evaluation. The appropriate test depends on your symptoms and clinical circumstances.

If H. pylori is confirmed, treatment generally involves specific medicines prescribed by a healthcare professional to eliminate the infection and reduce the risk of related complications.

## Step 2: Medicines May Be Used

Treatment varies according to the cause.

Doctors may recommend medicines that reduce stomach acid, particularly when acid-related irritation or another related digestive condition is present.

Depending on the situation, these can include acid-reducing medicines such as proton pump inhibitors or H2-receptor blockers.

If a medication is contributing to stomach irritation, your doctor may review whether it should be changed, reduced, or replaced.

Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own, even if you think it is causing stomach symptoms.

## Step 3: Make Stomach-Friendly Lifestyle Changes

Lifestyle measures may help reduce irritation and improve digestive symptoms, although they do not replace treatment for an underlying infection or other medical condition.

Helpful measures may include:

- Limiting alcohol if it worsens your symptoms

- Avoiding foods that consistently trigger discomfort

- Eating regular, balanced meals

- Avoiding very large meals if they aggravate symptoms

- Taking medicines exactly as prescribed

- Avoiding unnecessary use of NSAID painkillers

There is no single "gastritis diet" that works for everyone. Identifying your own triggers is often more useful than unnecessarily eliminating large numbers of foods.

## What About Spicy Foods?

Spicy or acidic foods may worsen symptoms for some people, but they are not necessarily the underlying cause of gastritis.

If a particular food consistently causes discomfort, reducing it may help you feel better.

## When Might More Testing Be Needed?

Some people with persistent or concerning symptoms may need further investigation.

An upper gastrointestinal endoscopy uses a flexible camera to examine the esophagus, stomach, and upper part of the small intestine. It may be considered when symptoms warrant a closer examination or when the diagnosis is uncertain.

The need for endoscopy depends on factors such as symptoms, age, medical history, and clinical findings.

## When Should You Seek Medical Attention?

Do not assume persistent stomach pain is simply gastritis.

:::emergency

Seek urgent medical attention if you vomit blood, pass black or bloody stools, experience severe or rapidly worsening abdominal pain, faint, or develop severe weakness or dizziness.

:::

Persistent vomiting, unexplained weight loss, difficulty swallowing, or recurring abdominal pain should also be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

## Final Thoughts

Gastritis can often be managed effectively once the underlying cause is identified. Treatment may involve addressing H. pylori infection, reviewing medications, reducing stomach irritation, or using acid-reducing medicines when appropriate. Lifestyle changes can support recovery and help control symptoms, but they should not replace medical evaluation when symptoms persist. If your stomach pain keeps returning or you develop warning signs such as bleeding, severe pain, vomiting, or unexplained weight loss, seek professional medical advice.
