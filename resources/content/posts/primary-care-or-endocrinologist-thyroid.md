When you are diagnosed with a thyroid problem, you may wonder whether you need a specialist. Primary care and internal medicine doctors manage many thyroid conditions well, while endocrinologists, who specialise in hormones, help with complex or difficult cases.

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Thyroid Disorders Explained: Hypothyroidism vs. Hyperthyroidism](/health-library/hypothyroidism-vs-hyperthyroidism-symptoms/).

## What Can a Primary Care or Internal Medicine Doctor Do?

- Assess symptoms and order thyroid tests

- Diagnose common conditions such as hypothyroidism

- Start and adjust levothyroxine

- Monitor thyroid levels regularly

- Review medicines and supplements

- Manage related conditions such as diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol

An internist sees the whole picture, which helps because thyroid disease often occurs alongside other health problems.

## What Does an Endocrinologist Do?

An endocrinologist specialises in the endocrine system, including the thyroid, pituitary, adrenal glands and pancreas. They manage complex thyroid conditions, hyperthyroidism, thyroid nodules, thyroid eye disease and unusual hormone patterns.

## When Is an Endocrinologist Referral Helpful?

- Hyperthyroidism needing specialist treatment such as radioactive iodine

- Thyroid nodules or goitre that need evaluation

- Thyroid levels that stay abnormal despite treatment

- Unclear or unusual test results

- Thyroid disease in pregnancy or planning pregnancy, depending on complexity

- Thyroid eye disease

- Suspected pituitary or other hormone disorders

A referral does not mean the condition is serious. It means specialist input may help.

## Can Both Doctors Work Together?

Yes. Often an endocrinologist advises on a plan, and the primary care doctor continues long-term monitoring and manages other conditions.

## How Should You Take Thyroid Medication?

Take levothyroxine as directed, usually on an empty stomach with water, and keep a gap from calcium, iron and some other medicines. Never change or stop the dose without advice.

## How Do You Choose?

For straightforward, stable thyroid conditions, a primary care or internal medicine doctor is usually suitable. For complex or difficult cases, an endocrinologist adds expertise. What matters most is consistent follow-up and a doctor who knows your full health picture.
