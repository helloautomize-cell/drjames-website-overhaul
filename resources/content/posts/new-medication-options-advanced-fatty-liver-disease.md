## Are There New Medicines for Fatty Liver Disease?

Yes. Treatment for [fatty liver disease](/health-library/fatty-liver-disease-causes-symptoms-and-how-to-reverse-it/) has evolved, particularly for people with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) and significant liver fibrosis.

It is important to understand that not everyone with fatty liver needs medication. Treatment depends on the type of liver disease, the degree of fibrosis, associated metabolic conditions, and overall health.

The newer approach focuses not only on reducing liver fat but also on addressing liver inflammation and fibrosis, which are important factors in the progression of liver disease.

## Why Does Liver Fibrosis Matter?

Fat accumulation in the liver does not automatically mean that advanced liver disease is present.

Some people develop inflammation and liver-cell injury, known as MASH. Continued inflammation can lead to fibrosis, which means scar tissue develops in the liver.

Advanced fibrosis can increase the risk of complications, which is why doctors may use blood tests, imaging, and other assessments to determine the degree of liver scarring.

Knowing the stage of liver disease is important before considering whether medication is appropriate.

## Resmetirom: A Newer Treatment Option

Resmetirom is a medication developed specifically for certain adults with noncirrhotic MASH and moderate-to-advanced liver fibrosis.

It works on thyroid hormone pathways in the liver and is intended to help address processes involved in liver fat and fibrosis.

However, it is not a treatment for every person with [fatty liver disease](/conditions/fatty-liver/). Eligibility depends on factors such as the diagnosis, degree of fibrosis, and whether cirrhosis is present.

Because it can interact with other medicines and may affect certain laboratory values, treatment requires appropriate medical supervision.

## Medication Is Not a Replacement for Lifestyle Care

Even when medication is appropriate, it should be viewed as part of a broader treatment plan.

Managing body weight, blood glucose, cholesterol, blood pressure, diet, and physical activity remains important for people with metabolic fatty liver disease.

Your doctor may also review alcohol intake and other medicines or supplements that could affect liver health.

## What About Medicines for Diabetes and Obesity?

Some medications originally developed for type 2 diabetes or obesity can also improve metabolic factors associated with fatty liver disease.

For example, certain medicines that promote weight loss or improve blood glucose may reduce liver fat as part of their overall metabolic effects.

However, their suitability depends on your individual health. A medication should not be started simply because it is advertised as a “fatty liver treatment.”

## Not Every Patient Needs a New Drug

If you have simple liver fat without significant fibrosis, lifestyle and metabolic risk management may remain the main approach.

Similarly, advanced cirrhosis requires a different type of medical management, and medications approved for noncirrhotic disease may not be appropriate.

This is why accurate staging is essential.

## What Should Patients Ask Their Doctor?

If you have been diagnosed with advanced fatty liver disease, ask:

- Do I have MASH or another form of fatty liver disease?

- Do I have liver fibrosis, and how advanced is it?

- Do I qualify for medication?

- What are the expected benefits and possible risks?

- Could my current medicines interact with the treatment?

- How will my liver health be monitored?

- What lifestyle changes should I continue?

These questions can help you understand why a particular treatment is being recommended.

## When to See a Doctor

:::emergency

Ask your doctor about your liver scarring stage if you have fatty liver with diabetes, obesity or abnormal liver tests. Seek urgent care for yellow skin or eyes, vomiting blood, black stools, severe abdominal swelling or confusion.

:::

## Final Thoughts

Newer medicines have expanded treatment options for selected patients with advanced fatty liver disease, particularly those with MASH and significant fibrosis. However, these treatments are not suitable for everyone. Understanding your diagnosis and the degree of liver scarring is essential before deciding whether medication is appropriate. If you have been diagnosed with advanced fatty liver disease, discuss your test results and treatment options with a qualified healthcare professional rather than starting supplements or medications on your own.
