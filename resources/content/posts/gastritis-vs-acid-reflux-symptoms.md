## Gastritis and Acid Reflux Are Not the Same

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Abdominal Pain Causes & Gastritis: Symptoms and Treatment](/health-library/abdominal-pain-causes-gastritis-treatment/).

Gastritis and acid reflux are often confused because both can cause discomfort in the upper digestive system. However, they are different conditions.

Gastritis involves inflammation of the stomach lining, while acid reflux occurs when stomach contents flow backward into the esophagus, the tube connecting your mouth to your stomach.

Because their symptoms can overlap, the location and nature of your discomfort can provide clues, but they cannot confirm the diagnosis by themselves.

## Gastritis Symptoms: What Does It Feel Like?

Gastritis may cause pain or discomfort in the upper abdomen, often described as burning, aching, or gnawing.

Other possible symptoms include:

- Nausea

- Vomiting

- Bloating

- Indigestion

- Feeling full quickly

- Reduced appetite

Some people with gastritis may have few or no symptoms.

## What Causes Gastritis?

One important cause is Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection. Certain painkillers called nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), including ibuprofen and naproxen, can also irritate the stomach lining.

Other causes depend on the individual's health and circumstances.

Finding the underlying cause is important because treatment should address the reason for the stomach inflammation.

## Acid Reflux Symptoms: How Are They Different?

Acid reflux typically causes heartburn, a burning sensation behind the breastbone that may move upward toward the throat.

Other symptoms can include:

- Sour or bitter taste in the mouth

- Regurgitation of stomach contents

- Burning after meals

- Symptoms that become worse when lying down

- Difficulty swallowing in some cases

- Chronic cough or throat irritation in some people

Acid reflux can occur occasionally, but frequent or troublesome symptoms may indicate gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD).

## Why Does Acid Reflux Happen?

Acid reflux can occur when the barrier between the stomach and esophagus does not prevent stomach contents from moving upward effectively.

Certain foods, large meals, lying down soon after eating, excess body weight, pregnancy, and some medications can contribute to reflux symptoms in some people.

However, triggers vary between individuals.

## Gastritis vs Acid Reflux: The Main Difference

The simplest way to understand the difference is:

Gastritis affects the stomach lining. Acid reflux affects the movement of stomach contents into the esophagus.

Gastritis tends to cause upper abdominal discomfort, while acid reflux more commonly causes burning behind the breastbone or symptoms that travel toward the throat.

However, a person can have both conditions at the same time, and symptoms can overlap.

## How Do Doctors Tell Them Apart?

Your doctor may ask about the location, timing, duration, and nature of your symptoms.

They may also ask about medications, alcohol use, diet, previous digestive problems, and other health conditions.

Depending on the symptoms, testing for H. pylori may be considered. Some patients may require an upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, particularly when there are concerning symptoms or when the diagnosis remains uncertain.

Treatment should be based on the underlying condition rather than symptoms alone.

## When Should You See a Doctor?

Occasional mild indigestion or reflux may not require immediate medical attention. However, persistent or recurrent symptoms should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

:::emergency

Seek urgent medical attention for vomiting blood, black or bloody stools, severe or rapidly worsening [abdominal pain](/conditions/digestive-problems/), fainting, or severe weakness.

:::

:::emergency

Chest pain should also not automatically be assumed to be acid reflux. If chest discomfort is severe, new, or associated with breathlessness, sweating, nausea, or other concerning symptoms, seek urgent medical assessment.

:::

## Final Thoughts

Gastritis and acid reflux can feel similar, but they involve different parts and processes within the digestive system. Gastritis primarily affects the stomach lining, while acid reflux involves stomach contents moving back into the esophagus. If symptoms continue, repeatedly return, or interfere with your daily life, a medical evaluation can help determine the actual cause and guide appropriate treatment. Avoid assuming that every episode of upper abdominal or chest discomfort is simply "acidity."
