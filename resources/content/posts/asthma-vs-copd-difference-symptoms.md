## Asthma and COPD: Similar Symptoms, Different Conditions

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Asthma Symptoms and Triggers: Causes, Diagnosis & Relief](/health-library/asthma-symptoms-triggers-relief/).

[Asthma](/conditions/asthma-and-breathing/) and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) can both cause breathing difficulties. Wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, and shortness of breath may occur with either condition.

That similarity can make it difficult to know which condition is responsible for your symptoms.

However, asthma and COPD are not the same disease. They have different patterns, risk factors, and approaches to diagnosis and treatment. Getting the correct diagnosis is important because treatment should be based on the underlying condition.

## What Is Asthma?

Asthma is a chronic condition in which the airways become inflamed and narrowed.

Symptoms can vary over time and may be triggered by factors such as:

- Allergens

- Respiratory infections

- Exercise

- Cold air

- Smoke and air pollution

- Strong odors or chemical irritants

People with asthma may have periods when their breathing is normal and other times when symptoms become more noticeable.

Wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, and shortness of breath are common asthma symptoms.

## What Is COPD?

COPD is a long-term lung disease that causes persistent airflow limitation. It includes conditions such as emphysema and chronic bronchitis.

Smoking is an important risk factor, although COPD can also occur in people who have never smoked. Long-term exposure to air pollution, workplace dust or fumes, and certain other factors can contribute to COPD.

Unlike asthma, COPD commonly causes symptoms that gradually become persistent or worsen over time.

## Asthma vs COPD: The Main Differences

One useful way to understand the difference is to look at the pattern of symptoms.

- Typical symptom pattern: Asthma: Often varies over time. COPD: Often persistent and progressive.

- Common symptoms: Asthma: Wheezing, cough, chest tightness, breathlessness. COPD: Breathlessness, chronic cough, mucus production.

- Common triggers: Asthma: Allergens, infections, exercise, cold air, irritants. COPD: Smoking and long-term exposure to lung irritants.

- Airflow limitation: Asthma: Often variable. COPD: Usually persistent.

- Diagnosis: Asthma: Clinical assessment and lung function testing. COPD: Clinical assessment and lung function testing.

These are general patterns, not rules. Some people have features of both conditions, and symptoms alone cannot reliably distinguish them.

## Age and Medical History Can Provide Clues

Asthma can begin during childhood or adulthood. COPD is more commonly diagnosed later in life, particularly among people with significant exposure to tobacco smoke or other lung irritants.

However, age alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

Your smoking history, occupational exposures, allergies, previous respiratory problems, symptom pattern, and response to treatment may all be relevant.

## How Are Asthma and COPD Diagnosed?

Doctors generally use your medical history, symptoms, physical examination, and lung function testing.

Spirometry is an important breathing test that measures how much air you can exhale and how quickly you can do it.

Additional testing may be recommended depending on your symptoms and medical history.

Because asthma and COPD can resemble other conditions, persistent breathlessness or chronic cough should not simply be assumed to be asthma or COPD.

## Can Someone Have Both Asthma and COPD?

Yes. Some people have features of both conditions.

This can make diagnosis and treatment more complicated and is another reason not to rely on symptoms alone.

A healthcare professional can assess the overall clinical picture and determine the most appropriate treatment approach.

## When Should You See a Doctor?

Arrange a medical evaluation if you have recurring wheezing, persistent cough, unexplained shortness of breath, reduced exercise tolerance, or frequent breathing problems.

:::emergency

Severe or rapidly worsening breathing difficulty requires urgent medical attention.

:::

Do not wait for symptoms to become severe before seeking help.

## Final Thoughts

Asthma and COPD can look similar at first because both may cause coughing, wheezing, and shortness of breath. The key difference is the underlying pattern of airway and lung disease. Asthma often involves variable airway narrowing, while COPD generally causes persistent airflow limitation. If you regularly experience breathing problems, getting an accurate diagnosis is more useful than trying to determine the condition yourself. A medical evaluation and appropriate lung function testing can help guide the right treatment.
