## Why Am I Still Short of Breath After Using My Inhaler?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Asthma Symptoms and Triggers: Causes, Diagnosis & Relief](/health-library/asthma-symptoms-triggers-relief/).

If you use an inhaler for [asthma](/conditions/asthma-and-breathing/) but continue to feel short of breath, it does not necessarily mean that your inhaler is “not working.”

There can be several reasons for ongoing breathlessness. Your asthma may not be adequately controlled, the inhaler may not be reaching your lungs effectively, or your symptoms may have another cause.

The important point is that persistent breathing difficulty should not simply be ignored or treated by repeatedly increasing your inhaler use without medical advice.

## Your Asthma May Not Be Fully Controlled

Asthma symptoms can include wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, and shortness of breath.

If your airways remain inflamed or narrowed, you may continue to experience symptoms even when using medication.

Triggers such as respiratory infections, smoke, air pollution, allergens, exercise, or cold air can also make symptoms worse.

Your doctor may need to review your symptoms and current treatment to determine whether your asthma is adequately controlled.

## Your Inhaler Technique May Matter

Using an inhaler correctly is more important than many people realize.

If the medication is not inhaled properly, less of it may reach the airways.

Common technique problems can include incorrect timing between pressing the inhaler and breathing in, inhaling too quickly or too slowly for the particular device, or failing to follow the device's instructions.

A healthcare professional can watch your technique and show you how to use your specific inhaler correctly.

## You May Be Using the Wrong Type of Inhaler for the Problem

Different inhalers have different purposes.

Some provide quick relief of airway narrowing, while others are intended for regular treatment to reduce airway inflammation and prevent symptoms.

Using one type when another treatment is needed may leave symptoms inadequately controlled.

Do not change your inhaler, dose, or frequency on your own. Your treatment plan should be based on your diagnosis and symptoms.

## Your Breathlessness May Have Another Cause

Not every episode of shortness of breath is caused by asthma.

Conditions such as COPD, respiratory infections, heart problems, anaemia, anxiety, or other medical conditions can cause breathlessness.

Some people may also have more than one condition at the same time.

This is why persistent symptoms deserve reassessment rather than simply assuming that asthma is responsible.

## Could It Be COPD or Another Lung Condition?

COPD can cause ongoing breathlessness, coughing, and reduced exercise tolerance. It is more common in people with significant exposure to tobacco smoke or other lung irritants.

If your symptoms are persistent, particularly if they have gradually worsened over time, your doctor may consider other lung conditions.

## Lung Function Testing Can Help

Spirometry is a common breathing test that measures how much air you can breathe out and how quickly you can do so.

Depending on your symptoms and medical history, your doctor may recommend spirometry or other investigations to clarify the cause of your breathlessness.

## When Should You Get Urgent Help?

Do not rely on an inhaler alone if you are experiencing severe or rapidly worsening breathing difficulty.

:::emergency

Seek urgent medical attention if you are struggling to breathe, cannot speak normally because of breathlessness, develop bluish or grey lips or skin, become confused or faint, or have severe chest pain or pressure.

:::

:::emergency

If your prescribed rescue treatment is not providing the expected relief and your breathing remains severely difficult, seek urgent care.

:::

## What Should You Do If Breathlessness Keeps Returning?

If you regularly feel short of breath despite using your inhaler, arrange a medical review.

Your doctor may assess your symptoms, inhaler technique, medication use, possible triggers, lung function, and other health conditions.

Do not increase the frequency of your inhaler or start additional medication without professional advice.

## Final Thoughts

Being short of breath despite using an inhaler is a reason to reassess the situation, not simply to take more medication. The cause may be uncontrolled asthma, inhaler technique, ongoing triggers, or an entirely different medical condition. If breathlessness keeps returning or is getting worse, speak with a healthcare professional for a proper evaluation. If breathing becomes severe or rapidly deteriorates, seek urgent medical care.
