## What Does Gastritis Pain Feel Like?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Abdominal Pain Causes & Gastritis: Symptoms and Treatment](/health-library/abdominal-pain-causes-gastritis-treatment/).

Gastritis is inflammation of the stomach lining. It can cause discomfort in the upper abdomen, although some people with gastritis have few or no noticeable symptoms.

When symptoms occur, gastritis pain is often described as a burning, aching, gnawing, or uncomfortable sensation in the upper part of the abdomen.

The exact symptoms can vary depending on the underlying cause and whether another digestive condition is present.

## Where Does Gastritis Usually Hurt?

Gastritis pain most commonly occurs in the upper middle or upper-left area of the abdomen, beneath the breastbone and ribs.

Some people may describe the discomfort simply as "stomach pain" or indigestion rather than being able to identify a precise location.

Pain location alone cannot confirm gastritis because several other conditions can cause similar symptoms.

## Can Gastritis Pain Move?

The discomfort may sometimes feel more widespread or may be perceived in nearby areas. However, severe pain that spreads to the chest, back, or other areas should not automatically be assumed to be gastritis.

Depending on the symptoms, your doctor may need to consider other digestive or non-digestive causes.

## Common Symptoms of Gastritis

Gastritis can cause symptoms such as:

- Upper [abdominal pain](/conditions/digestive-problems/) or discomfort

- Burning or aching stomach pain

- Nausea

- Vomiting

- Feeling full quickly

- Bloating

- Loss of appetite

- Indigestion

Some people may develop gastritis without obvious symptoms, particularly when it is discovered during an evaluation for another reason.

## What Causes Gastritis?

Gastritis can have several causes.

One important cause is Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection, a bacterial infection that can affect the stomach lining.

Certain pain-relieving medicines, particularly nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen and naproxen, can also irritate the stomach lining in some people.

Other factors may contribute to gastritis depending on the individual, including alcohol use and certain illnesses or physical stresses on the body.

Identifying the cause matters because treatment depends on what is responsible for the inflammation.

## Gastritis and Painkillers

Regular use of certain painkillers can increase the risk of stomach irritation and related problems.

If you regularly take pain medication and develop persistent stomach pain, nausea, or other digestive symptoms, discuss this with your doctor. Do not stop an essential prescribed medicine without medical advice.

## How Is Gastritis Diagnosed?

Your doctor may ask about your symptoms, medications, medical history, and other possible risk factors.

Depending on the situation, testing for H. pylori may be recommended.

Some patients may require an upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, which uses a flexible camera to examine the upper digestive tract. Whether this is necessary depends on symptoms, age, medical history, and clinical assessment.

## When Is Stomach Pain a Warning Sign?

:::emergency

Not every episode of stomach discomfort requires urgent care. However, certain symptoms should never be ignored.

:::

:::emergency

Seek urgent medical attention if you vomit blood, pass black or bloody stools, experience severe or rapidly worsening abdominal pain, faint, become unusually weak, or develop significant dizziness.

:::

Persistent vomiting, unexplained weight loss, difficulty swallowing, or ongoing abdominal pain should also be discussed with a healthcare professional.

## Don't Assume Every Stomach Ache Is Gastritis

Gastritis can cause upper abdominal discomfort, but similar symptoms may occur with ulcers, reflux, gallbladder problems, pancreatitis, infections, and other conditions.

If your symptoms continue or repeatedly return, getting the correct diagnosis is more useful than repeatedly treating the pain on your own.

## Final Thoughts

Gastritis can cause burning or aching discomfort in the upper abdomen, but the symptoms are not always specific. Where the pain occurs and how it feels can provide useful clues, but they cannot establish the diagnosis by themselves. If stomach pain is persistent, recurrent, or accompanied by warning signs such as bleeding, vomiting, weight loss, or severe pain, medical evaluation is important. Identifying the underlying cause allows treatment to be directed at the actual problem rather than simply masking the symptoms.
