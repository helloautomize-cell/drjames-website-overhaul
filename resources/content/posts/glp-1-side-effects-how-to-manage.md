GLP-1 medicines such as semaglutide and tirzepatide work partly by slowing stomach emptying and reducing appetite. These same effects cause the most common side effects, mainly digestive symptoms. Most are mild to moderate and improve over time, especially when doses are increased slowly.

For the wider picture, see our guide on [GLP-1 for Diabetes & Weight Loss: Results, Cost & Side Effects](/health-library/glp-1-diabetes-weight-loss-results-cost-side-effects/).

## What Are the Common Side Effects?

- Nausea and occasional vomiting

- Diarrhoea or constipation

- Bloating, burping and indigestion

- Reduced appetite and early fullness

- Tiredness or mild headache

- Injection site reactions

## How Can You Manage Nausea?

- Eat smaller, slower meals and stop at the first sign of fullness

- Avoid fatty, fried and very spicy foods

- Choose bland foods when nauseous

- Avoid lying down straight after eating

- Sip fluids between meals

## How Can You Manage Constipation?

- Drink plenty of water

- Eat fibre-rich foods such as vegetables, fruits, pulses and whole grains

- Stay physically active

- Ask your doctor about a gentle laxative if needed

## How Can You Manage Diarrhoea?

- Drink fluids and oral rehydration solutions if needed

- Eat simple foods and avoid greasy meals

- Contact your doctor if it is severe or persistent

## Why Does Dose Matter?

Doses are increased gradually to let the body adjust. If side effects are troublesome, your doctor may slow the increase or keep you at a lower dose for longer. Do not increase the dose on your own.

## What Are the Rare but Serious Risks?

- Pancreatitis: severe abdominal pain that may spread to the back, with or without vomiting

- Gallbladder problems: pain in the upper right abdomen, fever or yellowing of skin

- Dehydration and kidney problems from vomiting or diarrhoea

- Low blood sugar when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas

- Allergic reactions

- GLP-1 medicines are not recommended for people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2 syndrome

## When Should You Call Your Doctor?

:::emergency

Call your doctor or seek urgent care for severe or persistent abdominal pain, vomiting that prevents you keeping fluids down, signs of dehydration, yellow eyes or skin, symptoms of low blood sugar, or swelling of the face or difficulty breathing.

:::
