Ashwagandha is an herbal supplement from Ayurvedic tradition, often marketed for stress, sleep and energy. It is widely available in Oman and online. If you have a [thyroid condition](/conditions/thyroid-disorders/) or take thyroid medication, it is important to know that ashwagandha may affect thyroid hormone levels.

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Thyroid Disorders Explained: Hypothyroidism vs. Hyperthyroidism](/health-library/hypothyroidism-vs-hyperthyroidism-symptoms/).

## How Might Ashwagandha Affect the Thyroid?

Some research has found that ashwagandha can raise thyroid hormone levels in certain people. The studies are small, use different products and doses, and are not enough to recommend ashwagandha as a thyroid treatment. Because even small changes in thyroid hormone matter for people on replacement therapy, caution is sensible.

## What If You Take Levothyroxine?

Levothyroxine is the standard treatment for hypothyroidism. Adding a supplement that may raise thyroid hormone could make it harder to keep levels in the right range. Your doctor may need to check thyroid tests more often if you choose to take ashwagandha.

## What About Hyperthyroidism?

People with an overactive thyroid, such as Graves' disease, should be particularly cautious, because a supplement that may increase thyroid hormone could worsen symptoms.

## What Are Possible Warning Signs?

Seek medical advice if you develop palpitations, a fast or irregular heartbeat, unexplained weight loss, tremor, excessive sweating, heat intolerance, anxiety or sleeplessness while taking ashwagandha. These symptoms have many causes, but should be checked.

## Are There Other Safety Concerns?

Rare cases of liver injury have been reported with ashwagandha. It may also interact with sedatives, immune-suppressing medicines and diabetes or blood pressure medicines. It is generally not recommended in pregnancy. Supplement quality and content can vary between brands.

## What Should You Do Before Taking It?

- Tell your doctor about all supplements and medicines

- Ask whether ashwagandha is suitable for your condition

- If you start, consider repeating thyroid tests as advised

- Do not use it as a replacement for prescribed treatment

- Stop and seek advice if new symptoms develop
