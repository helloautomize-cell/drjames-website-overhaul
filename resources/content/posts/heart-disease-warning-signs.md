The heart almost never fails without warning. The problem is that its warnings are usually quiet, gradual, and easy to explain away as age, weight or stress. This article covers the signs worth taking seriously, the ones easy to miss, and the ones that mean call 9999 now.

## The classic warning sign: chest discomfort on effort

The warning sign most people know is angina: a pressure, tightness or heaviness in the centre of the chest that appears with effort, climbing stairs, walking uphill, carrying bags, and settles with rest. It can spread to the left arm, shoulder, neck or jaw.

<!-- Wording to be approved by Dr. James. -->
:::margin
Most patients describe it as pressure, not pain.
:::

People often describe it as "someone sitting on my chest" rather than pain, which is why it gets ignored. Any chest discomfort that reliably comes with effort and eases with rest deserves a physician's assessment within days, not months.

## The quiet signs people explain away

Several early signs get blamed on other things for months:

- **Breathlessness on effort** that is new for you, or appearing earlier on the same walk.
- **Unusual fatigue**, especially a clear drop in what you could do three months ago.
- **Swelling in the feet or ankles** that pits when pressed, or shoes feeling tight by evening.
- **Palpitations**, a racing, fluttering or skipping heartbeat, particularly with dizziness.
- **Breathlessness lying flat** or waking at night gasping, needing extra pillows.
- **Effort-related indigestion**, burning or discomfort in the upper abdomen that tracks with exertion, common in people with diabetes.

None of these proves heart disease. Each becomes meaningful when it is new, progressive, or appears alongside the risk factors: high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, extra weight or a family history of early heart events.

## Signs that need an emergency call

Heart attack symptoms are different from the quiet signs. Call 9999 immediately for:

- Heavy, crushing chest pain lasting more than a few minutes, or returning.
- Pain spreading to the arm, jaw, back or upper abdomen.
- Chest pain with sweating, sickness, breathlessness or a sense of doom.
- Sudden collapse, or new weakness on one side with chest symptoms.

<!-- Wording to be approved by Dr. James. -->
:::emergency
Heavy chest pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back, with sweating or sickness: call 9999. Do not drive yourself.
:::

Do not drive yourself, and do not wait to see whether it settles. In a heart attack, minutes change outcomes.

## What raises the odds

The warning signs matter most when the background risk is there. The main drivers are the same few: high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes and prediabetes, smoking, excess weight, poor sleep and a family history of early heart disease. None of these cause symptoms on their own, which is why checking them while you feel well is the real protection.

## What we check at a risk assessment

A heart risk assessment puts your numbers together: blood pressure, cholesterol profile, sugar or HbA1c, kidney function, weight and waist, plus your family history and habits. An ECG heart tracing is added when indicated. The result is a plain answer about your risk and which changes would protect you most.

If you recognise yourself in the quiet signs, or you have never had your numbers measured, book a consultation. It is far better to hear "your heart is fine" than to guess.
