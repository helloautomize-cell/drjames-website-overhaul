GLP-1 medicines are a group of treatments that mimic a natural gut hormone called glucagon-like peptide-1. They help lower blood sugar, slow stomach emptying and reduce appetite. Medicines such as semaglutide, and the dual GIP/GLP-1 medicine tirzepatide, are used for [type 2 diabetes](/conditions/diabetes-and-prediabetes/) and, in some formulations, for weight management. For years they were difficult to obtain and expensive. That is gradually changing.

For the wider picture, see our guide on [GLP-1 for Diabetes & Weight Loss: Results, Cost & Side Effects](/health-library/glp-1-diabetes-weight-loss-results-cost-side-effects/).

## Why Were GLP-1 Medicines Hard to Get?

Demand rose sharply when these medicines showed significant weight-loss effects. Manufacturers struggled to keep up, causing shortages. Prices were high, and insurance coverage, especially for weight loss, was limited in many regions.

## What Has Changed?

### Improved supply

Manufacturers have expanded production capacity, easing shortages in many markets.

### More options

Additional brands, doses and delivery formats have become available, including weekly injections and oral tablets.

### Generic and biosimilar competition

As patents on some earlier GLP-1 molecules expire in different countries, lower-cost versions are becoming available, increasing competition.

### Wider awareness

More doctors are familiar with prescribing and monitoring these medicines.

## What Still Affects Cost?

- The specific medicine, brand and dose

- Whether it is prescribed for diabetes or weight management

- Insurance coverage and employer plans

- Country-specific regulation and pricing

- Treatment duration, since many people need long-term use

Ask your doctor and pharmacist about options available locally.

## How Can You Stay Safe?

Popularity has led to counterfeit and unregulated products sold online or through informal channels. These may contain the wrong dose, wrong ingredient or contaminants.

- Use only medicines prescribed by a licensed doctor

- Buy from licensed pharmacies

- Be wary of very low prices, social media sellers or products without proper labelling

- Do not share pens or doses with others

## Who May Be Suitable?

Doctors consider GLP-1 medicines for adults with type 2 diabetes and, depending on local approvals, for adults with obesity or overweight with related health conditions. They are not suitable for everyone, including people with certain thyroid cancer histories, a history of pancreatitis in some cases, or during pregnancy.
