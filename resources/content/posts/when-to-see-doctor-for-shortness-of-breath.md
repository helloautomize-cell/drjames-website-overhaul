## What Does Shortness of Breath Mean?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Asthma Symptoms and Triggers: Causes, Diagnosis & Relief](/health-library/asthma-symptoms-triggers-relief/).

Shortness of breath, also called breathlessness or dyspnea, is the feeling that you cannot get enough air or are having difficulty breathing.

It can happen for a simple reason, such as intense exercise, but it can also be caused by conditions affecting the lungs, heart, blood, or other parts of the body.

The important question is not only how severe the breathlessness feels, but also when it happens, how long it lasts, and whether it is getting worse.

## When Is Shortness of Breath Normal?

It is normal to breathe harder after strenuous exercise, especially if you are not accustomed to that level of activity.

Breathing should generally settle after you stop and rest.

However, breathlessness that occurs with routine activities, repeatedly wakes you at night, or appears without an obvious reason deserves medical attention.

## Pay Attention to Changes in Your Breathing

You should consider speaking with a doctor if you notice:

- Breathlessness during normal daily activities

- Increasing difficulty walking or climbing stairs

- Recurrent wheezing

- Persistent cough

- Chest tightness

- Shortness of breath when lying down

- Waking at night because you cannot breathe comfortably

- A noticeable decline in your usual exercise tolerance

These symptoms can have many possible causes, so they should not automatically be attributed to [asthma](/conditions/asthma-and-breathing/) or poor fitness.

## Common Causes of Shortness of Breath

Breathing difficulty can result from several conditions.

## Lung-Related Causes

Asthma, COPD, respiratory infections, pneumonia, and other lung conditions can cause breathlessness.

Asthma may cause episodes of wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, and shortness of breath. COPD may cause persistent breathlessness, particularly with activity.

## Heart-Related Causes

Some heart conditions can make it difficult to breathe, especially during physical activity or when lying down.

Shortness of breath accompanied by chest discomfort, swelling of the legs, or unusual fatigue should be medically assessed.

## Other Possible Causes

anaemia, anxiety or panic attacks, certain medications, reduced fitness, and other medical conditions can also cause or contribute to breathlessness.

Because the causes are so varied, identifying the underlying problem is more useful than simply treating the symptom.

## When Is Shortness of Breath an Emergency?

Some breathing problems require immediate medical attention.

:::emergency

Seek emergency care if you experience severe or rapidly worsening difficulty breathing, especially if it is accompanied by:

- Chest pain or pressure

- Fainting or severe dizziness

- Confusion

- Bluish or grey lips or skin

- Severe weakness

- Difficulty speaking because you are too breathless

- Sudden breathlessness that is severe or unexpected

:::

Do not wait for severe symptoms to improve on their own.

## What Will a Doctor Check?

Your doctor will usually begin by asking when the breathlessness occurs, how long you have had it, whether it is worsening, and whether you have other symptoms.

Depending on your situation, the evaluation may include checking your oxygen level, heart rate, blood pressure, and lungs.

Further tests may include spirometry, chest imaging, blood tests, an electrocardiogram, or other investigations when clinically appropriate.

The tests depend on the suspected cause.

## Don't Self-Diagnose Persistent Breathlessness

It is tempting to assume that shortness of breath is simply asthma, anxiety, age, or lack of fitness.

Sometimes it is, but not always.

Persistent or unexplained breathing difficulty deserves a proper medical assessment, particularly if it is new or getting worse.

## Final Thoughts

Shortness of breath is a symptom, not a diagnosis. While becoming breathless after strenuous exercise can be normal, unexplained, persistent, or worsening breathing difficulty should be evaluated by a healthcare professional. The cause may involve the lungs, heart, blood, medications, or other health conditions. If breathing becomes suddenly severe or is accompanied by chest pain, fainting, confusion, or other serious symptoms, seek emergency medical care immediately.
