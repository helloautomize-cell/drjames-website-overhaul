## What Are ALT and AST?

If a routine blood test shows elevated ALT or AST, it is understandable to feel concerned. But an abnormal liver enzyme result does not automatically mean that you have serious liver disease.

ALT (alanine aminotransferase) and AST (aspartate aminotransferase) are enzymes found inside cells. ALT is found mainly in the liver, while AST is also present in other tissues, including muscles and the heart.

When cells are injured or stressed, these enzymes can enter the bloodstream and their levels may rise.

## What Does a High ALT Mean?

ALT is more closely associated with the liver than AST. An elevated ALT can occur when liver cells are irritated or damaged.

Possible causes include:

- [Fatty liver disease](/health-library/fatty-liver-disease-causes-symptoms-and-how-to-reverse-it/)

- Viral hepatitis

- Alcohol-related liver injury

- Certain medicines or supplements

- Other liver conditions

However, the ALT number alone cannot identify the exact cause.

The degree of elevation, other blood-test results, medical history, medications, alcohol use, and symptoms all need to be considered.

## What Does a High AST Mean?

AST can also rise when liver cells are affected, but it is not specific to the liver.

Muscle injury or strenuous physical activity can sometimes contribute to an elevated AST. Other medical conditions can also affect the result.

This is why doctors usually interpret AST together with ALT and other liver tests rather than looking at AST in isolation.

## ALT vs AST: Why Both Matter

Looking at the pattern of liver-test abnormalities can provide more information than a single result.

Your doctor may also review tests such as bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, albumin, and other markers depending on your situation.

The pattern can help determine whether the results suggest liver-cell injury, a problem involving bile flow, or another cause.

## Does an Elevated Result Mean Liver Disease?

Not necessarily.

Temporary enzyme elevations can occur for different reasons, and some people with liver disease may have only mild abnormalities or even normal enzyme levels at a particular point in time.

For this reason, liver enzymes should not be used alone to determine the severity of liver disease.

Your doctor may repeat the blood tests, review your medicines and supplements, assess metabolic risk factors, or recommend imaging or additional testing when appropriate.

## What Should You Do If Your Liver Enzymes Are High?

Do not panic based on a single abnormal result.

Instead, discuss the result with your healthcare professional. Tell them about all medicines, supplements, and herbal products you take. Your doctor may also ask about alcohol intake, weight changes, diabetes, and other health conditions.

Do not stop prescribed medication without medical advice simply because your liver enzymes are elevated.

Seek medical attention promptly if abnormal liver tests occur with symptoms such as yellowing of the skin or eyes, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, confusion, or significant drowsiness.

## When to See a Doctor

:::emergency

Talk to your doctor if a routine test shows high ALT or AST, especially if the result persists. Seek urgent care for yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, severe abdominal pain or persistent vomiting.

:::

## Final Thoughts

An elevated ALT or AST result is a finding, not a diagnosis. While abnormal liver enzymes can sometimes indicate liver injury, they can also occur for other reasons. The most useful next step is to have the results interpreted in the context of your overall health, medications, symptoms, and other tests. If your liver enzymes remain elevated, appropriate evaluation can help identify the cause and determine whether further monitoring or treatment is needed.
