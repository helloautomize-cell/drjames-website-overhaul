## Who Should Manage Fatty Liver Disease?

Being told that you have [fatty liver disease](/health-library/fatty-liver-disease-causes-symptoms-and-how-to-reverse-it/) can raise an immediate question: “Do I need to see a liver specialist?”

Not necessarily.

Many people with [fatty liver](/conditions/fatty-liver/) can begin evaluation and management with a primary care or internal medicine doctor. These doctors can assess common risk factors, review blood tests and imaging, and address related conditions such as obesity, diabetes, high cholesterol, and high blood pressure.

A liver specialist may become important when there are signs of more advanced liver disease, uncertainty about the diagnosis, or a need for more specialised assessment.

## What Can a Primary Care Doctor Do?

Primary care is often the starting point after fatty liver is discovered on an ultrasound, blood test, or other evaluation.

Your doctor can review:

- Liver enzyme results such as ALT and AST

- Blood glucose and diabetes status

- Cholesterol and triglyceride levels

- Body weight and metabolic health

- Alcohol intake

- Medications and supplements

- Medical and family history

They can also help identify factors that may be contributing to liver fat and create a plan focused on improving overall metabolic health.

## Lifestyle Management Matters

For many people, managing fatty liver involves addressing the underlying metabolic factors rather than simply treating the liver itself.

Depending on your situation, your doctor may discuss nutrition, physical activity, weight management, diabetes control, and cholesterol or blood-pressure management.

These decisions should be individualized rather than based on a one-size-fits-all diet or supplement.

## When Is a Liver Specialist Needed?

A hepatologist is a doctor who specialised in liver diseases. Gastroenterologists also diagnose and manage many liver conditions.

Referral may be appropriate when there are concerns about liver scarring, unexplained or persistent liver abnormalities, more advanced disease, or uncertainty about the diagnosis.

A specialist may recommend additional evaluation to determine whether fatty liver has progressed beyond simple fat accumulation.

## Fatty Liver Is Not Always the Same Condition

The term fatty liver can describe a range of situations.

Today, you may hear MASLD, which stands for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. Some people can develop liver inflammation and progressive scarring, while others have little or no significant liver injury.

The key issue is not simply whether fat is present in the liver. Doctors also need to consider whether there is fibrosis, or liver scarring.

## Why Liver Scarring Matters

Advanced fibrosis increases the risk of complications from chronic liver disease.

Your doctor may use blood-test-based calculations, imaging, or other assessments to estimate the likelihood of significant fibrosis. If the results suggest increased risk, specialist evaluation may be appropriate.

## What If My Liver Enzymes Are Normal?

Normal liver enzymes do not always mean that the liver is completely healthy.

Some people with fatty liver and clinically important liver disease may not have markedly elevated ALT or AST levels. This is why your overall medical history and assessment matter rather than relying on a single blood test.

If fatty liver has been identified, ask your doctor whether your risk of liver fibrosis needs further evaluation.

## Primary Care and Liver Specialists Can Work Together

You do not necessarily have to choose one doctor permanently.

A primary care or internal medicine doctor can continue managing your broader health while a hepatologist or gastroenterologist provides specialist input when needed.

This team-based approach can be particularly useful when fatty liver occurs alongside diabetes, obesity, high cholesterol, or other metabolic conditions.

## When to See a Doctor

:::emergency

Book a visit if you have fatty liver, abnormal liver tests, diabetes or obesity. Ask about a specialist referral if tests suggest scarring or your results are not improving. Seek urgent care for yellow skin or eyes, vomiting blood or black stools.

:::

## Final Thoughts

A fatty liver diagnosis does not automatically mean you need a liver specialist. For many people, primary care or internal medicine is an appropriate place to begin evaluation and management. The important question is whether there are signs of liver injury or fibrosis that require more specialised assessment. Your doctor can review your risk factors and test results and determine whether monitoring, lifestyle-focused management, further testing, or referral to a liver specialist is appropriate.
