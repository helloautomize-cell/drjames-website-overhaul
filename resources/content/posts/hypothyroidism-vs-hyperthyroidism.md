The [thyroid](/conditions/thyroid-disorders/) is a small, butterfly-shaped gland at the front of the neck. It makes hormones that control metabolism, energy, heart rate and body temperature. Hypothyroidism means the thyroid makes too little hormone, while hyperthyroidism means it makes too much. Because they push the body in opposite directions, their symptoms often look very different.

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Thyroid Disorders Explained: Hypothyroidism vs. Hyperthyroidism](/health-library/hypothyroidism-vs-hyperthyroidism-symptoms/).

## How Do the Symptoms Compare?

| Feature | Hypothyroidism (underactive) | Hyperthyroidism (overactive) |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Tiredness, sluggishness | Restlessness, fatigue from poor sleep |
| Temperature | Feeling cold | Feeling hot, sweating |
| Weight | Weight gain | Weight loss despite good appetite |
| Heart | Slow heart rate | Fast or irregular heartbeat |
| Bowels | Constipation | Frequent bowel movements |
| Skin and hair | Dry skin, hair thinning | Warm, moist skin, hair thinning |
| Mood | Low mood, slowed thinking | Anxiety, irritability |
| Periods | Heavier or irregular | Lighter or irregular |
| Other | Puffy face, muscle aches | Tremor, eye changes in Graves' disease |

## What Causes Hypothyroidism?

Hashimoto's thyroiditis, an autoimmune condition, is a common cause. Other causes include previous thyroid surgery or radioactive iodine treatment, some medicines and, in some regions, iodine deficiency.

## What Causes Hyperthyroidism?

Graves' disease, another autoimmune condition, is a common cause. Thyroid nodules that make excess hormone, thyroid inflammation and taking too much thyroid medication can also cause it.

## How Are Thyroid Problems Diagnosed?

A TSH test is usually the first step. TSH is a hormone from the pituitary gland that tells the thyroid how much to work.

- High TSH with low free T4 suggests hypothyroidism

- Low TSH with high free T4 or T3 suggests hyperthyroidism

Doctors may add antibody tests or an ultrasound depending on findings.

## Why Can't Symptoms Alone Confirm It?

Tiredness, weight change, anxiety and sleep problems have many causes, including stress, anaemia, diabetes and mental health conditions. Blood tests separate thyroid problems from these.

## How Are They Treated?

Hypothyroidism is usually treated with daily levothyroxine, adjusted using blood tests. Hyperthyroidism may be treated with antithyroid medicines, radioactive iodine or surgery, depending on cause. Beta blockers can ease palpitations and tremor while treatment takes effect.

## When Should You See a Doctor?

:::emergency

See a doctor for persistent changes in energy, weight, heart rate, temperature tolerance, bowel habits or mood. Seek urgent care for chest pain, fainting, a very fast or irregular heartbeat, severe breathlessness or confusion.

:::
