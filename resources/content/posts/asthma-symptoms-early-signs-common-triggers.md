## What Does Asthma Feel Like?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Asthma Symptoms and Triggers: Causes, Diagnosis & Relief](/health-library/asthma-symptoms-triggers-relief/).

[Asthma](/conditions/asthma-and-breathing/) is a chronic condition that affects the airways, the tubes that carry air into and out of the lungs. In people with asthma, the airways can become inflamed and narrowed, making breathing more difficult.

Asthma symptoms can vary in frequency and severity. Some people have occasional symptoms, while others experience symptoms more regularly.

Recognizing the early signs can help you seek medical advice before symptoms become more difficult to control.

## Common Symptoms of Asthma

The most common asthma symptoms include:

- Wheezing or a whistling sound while breathing

- Shortness of breath

- Chest tightness or pressure

- Coughing, particularly at night or early in the morning

A cough may sometimes be the main symptom, particularly in people with a form of asthma called cough-variant asthma.

Symptoms may become more noticeable during exercise, at night, during respiratory infections, or after exposure to a particular trigger.

## Symptoms Can Come and Go

Asthma is not necessarily associated with constant breathing difficulty.

A person may feel completely well between episodes. This can sometimes lead people to ignore occasional wheezing or coughing.

However, recurring symptoms are worth discussing with a healthcare professional, particularly if they interfere with sleep, exercise, work, or daily activities.

## Early Signs You Should Pay Attention To

Early asthma symptoms can be subtle.

You might notice that you cough repeatedly after exercising, become unusually short of breath around certain triggers, or experience occasional chest tightness.

Nighttime coughing or waking because of breathing difficulty can also be an important clue.

These symptoms do not automatically mean you have asthma. Other conditions can cause similar breathing problems, so proper medical assessment is important.

## Common Asthma Triggers

Asthma triggers differ from person to person.

Common triggers can include:

## Allergens

Some people experience symptoms after exposure to allergens such as pollen, dust mites, mold, or animal dander.

## Respiratory Infections

Colds and other respiratory infections can trigger asthma symptoms or make existing symptoms worse.

## Exercise and Cold Air

Physical activity can trigger symptoms in some people. Cold or dry air may also irritate sensitive airways.

## Smoke and Irritants

Tobacco smoke, air pollution, strong odors, chemical fumes, and other airborne irritants can contribute to symptoms.

## Stress and Other Factors

Emotional stress, certain medications, workplace exposures, and other factors may trigger symptoms in some individuals.

Identifying your personal triggers can be useful when developing an asthma management plan.

## When Are Asthma Symptoms an Emergency?

:::emergency

Most asthma symptoms can be managed with an appropriate treatment plan, but a severe asthma attack can become a medical emergency.

Seek urgent medical attention if breathing becomes severely difficult, symptoms are rapidly worsening, you cannot speak normally because of breathlessness, or your prescribed rescue treatment is not providing the expected relief.

:::

Do not wait for severe breathing difficulty to resolve on its own.

## How Is Asthma Diagnosed?

Asthma is diagnosed using your symptoms, medical history, physical examination, and, when appropriate, breathing tests.

Spirometry is a common lung function test that measures how much air you can breathe out and how quickly you can do so.

Your doctor may recommend additional testing depending on your age, symptoms, and clinical situation.

Because asthma symptoms can resemble other respiratory conditions, self-diagnosis is not recommended.

## Managing Asthma Starts With Understanding Your Symptoms

If you regularly experience coughing, wheezing, chest tightness, or shortness of breath, keep track of when symptoms occur and what seems to trigger them.

This information can help your doctor determine whether asthma is likely and develop an appropriate management plan.

Do not change or stop prescribed asthma medication without discussing it with your healthcare professional.

## Final Thoughts

Asthma symptoms are not always obvious, especially when they occur only occasionally. Recurrent wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, or shortness of breath can be important clues, even if you feel normal between episodes. Identifying triggers and getting an appropriate medical evaluation can help determine whether asthma is responsible for your symptoms. If breathing becomes severely difficult or symptoms worsen rapidly, seek urgent medical care rather than waiting for them to improve.
