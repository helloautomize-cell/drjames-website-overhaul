## Do Kidney Cleanses Really Detox Your Kidneys?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Kidney Health 101: Warning Signs You Shouldn’t Overlook](/health-library/kidney-health-101-warning-signs-you-shouldnt-overlook/).

Kidney cleanse products are often marketed as a simple way to remove toxins, flush waste, or “reset” the kidneys. They may come as teas, juices, powders, capsules, or herbal supplements.

But your kidneys already have an important natural filtering role. Healthy kidneys continuously remove waste and excess fluid from the blood and help maintain the body's fluid, electrolyte, and acid-base balance.

There is not good evidence that commercial “kidney cleanses” can detoxify healthy kidneys or reverse established [kidney disease](/conditions/kidney-problems/).

## How Do Your Kidneys Clean Your Blood?

The kidneys contain millions of tiny filtering structures called nephrons. Each nephron filters blood and helps determine which substances should remain in the body and which should leave through urine.

This process happens continuously. Your kidneys do not need a special tea or supplement to perform their normal filtering function.

If kidney function is reduced because of disease, a cleanse cannot simply restore damaged kidney tissue.

## What About Kidney Cleanse Teas and Supplements?

Many kidney cleanse products contain herbs, vitamins, or other ingredients promoted for urinary or kidney health.

The problem is that “natural” does not automatically mean safe or effective. Supplements can interact with medicines, contain multiple active ingredients, or cause unwanted effects.

Some products may also have diuretic effects, meaning they increase urination. More urination does not necessarily mean that the kidneys are being “detoxed.”

If you have kidney disease, diabetes, high blood pressure, or take regular medication, discuss supplements with your healthcare professional before using them.

## Can Drinking More Water Clean the Kidneys?

Adequate hydration is important for normal kidney function, but drinking excessive amounts of water does not provide an extra “detox” benefit.

Your fluid needs depend on factors such as your health, activity, environment, and kidney and heart function. People with certain medical conditions may even need to limit fluid intake.

The goal is appropriate hydration, not forcing large amounts of water.

## What Actually Supports Kidney Health?

Instead of relying on cleanses, focus on habits that protect kidney function over time:

- Keep blood pressure under appropriate control

- Manage blood glucose if you have diabetes

- Maintain a healthy weight

- Stay physically active

- Avoid unnecessary or excessive use of medicines that can affect the kidneys

- Follow medical advice if you already have kidney disease

- Have kidney function checked when recommended by your doctor

These measures address important risk factors for kidney disease rather than promising a quick detox.

## When Should You Get Your Kidneys Checked?

Kidney disease can develop without obvious symptoms. Testing may be particularly important if you have diabetes, high blood pressure, a family history of kidney disease, or other risk factors.

Blood and urine tests can help assess kidney function and identify abnormalities such as protein in the urine.

## Final Thoughts

Kidney cleanses may sound appealing, but there is no need to “flush” healthy kidneys with special teas, juices, or supplements. Your kidneys already perform their filtering role continuously. Rather than relying on detox products, focus on proven health habits and appropriate medical monitoring. If you are concerned about your kidney function, proper blood and urine testing is far more useful than a commercial cleanse.
