## What Can Cause Abdominal Pain?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Abdominal Pain Causes & Gastritis: Symptoms and Treatment](/health-library/abdominal-pain-causes-gastritis-treatment/).

[Abdominal pain](/conditions/digestive-problems/) is a common symptom, but its cause is not always obvious. Pain can come from the stomach, intestines, gallbladder, liver, pancreas, kidneys, urinary tract, or other structures inside or around the abdomen.

The location, timing, severity, and associated symptoms can provide useful clues. However, these features alone cannot reliably diagnose a condition.

:::emergency

Mild abdominal discomfort may result from indigestion or constipation, while sudden or severe pain can sometimes indicate a condition requiring urgent medical attention.

:::

## Abdominal Pain by Location

## Upper Abdomen

Pain in the upper abdomen may be associated with digestive problems such as indigestion, gastritis, or an ulcer. Pain in the upper right abdomen can also occur with gallbladder or liver problems.

Upper abdominal pain that is severe, persistent, or associated with vomiting, fever, jaundice, or chest discomfort should be medically assessed.

## Lower Right Abdomen

Pain in the lower right abdomen can have several causes. Appendicitis is an important condition doctors consider, particularly when pain becomes progressively worse and is accompanied by nausea, vomiting, fever, or loss of appetite.

Other digestive, urinary, or reproductive conditions can cause pain in the same area.

## Lower Left Abdomen

Pain on the lower left side can occur with constipation or other bowel problems. In some adults, particularly older adults, diverticular disease or diverticulitis may cause localized lower abdominal pain, sometimes accompanied by fever or changes in bowel habits.

## Pain Around the Middle of the Abdomen

Pain around the belly button can occur with several digestive conditions. In some cases of appendicitis, discomfort may initially begin around the navel before moving toward the lower right abdomen.

Location can change as a condition develops, which is one reason persistent or worsening pain should not be self-diagnosed.

## Timing and Pattern Can Provide Clues

The timing of abdominal pain can also be useful.

Pain that occurs after eating may be related to digestion, while cramping that comes and goes can occur with bowel problems. Pain that repeatedly wakes you from sleep or continues for a prolonged period deserves medical attention.

Sudden, severe abdominal pain is particularly important because some urgent conditions can begin abruptly.

## Other Symptoms Matter

Doctors will usually ask about symptoms occurring alongside the pain, such as:

- Fever

- Vomiting

- Diarrhea or constipation

- Bloating

- Loss of appetite

- Blood in the stool or vomit

- Painful urination

- Yellowing of the skin or eyes

- Unexplained weight loss

These details can help narrow down the possible causes.

## When Is Abdominal Pain an Emergency?

:::emergency

Not every stomach ache requires emergency care, but certain symptoms should not be ignored.

:::

:::emergency

Seek urgent medical attention for severe or rapidly worsening abdominal pain, significant abdominal swelling, persistent vomiting, vomiting blood, black or bloody stools, fainting, confusion, or severe weakness.

:::

Severe abdominal pain during pregnancy or pain accompanied by chest pain or difficulty breathing also requires prompt medical assessment.

## How Is the Cause Diagnosed?

A doctor may ask when the pain started, where it is located, whether it moves, what makes it better or worse, and which other symptoms are present.

The physical examination may be followed by blood or urine tests, ultrasound, X-ray, CT, or other investigations when clinically appropriate.

The right test depends on the suspected cause rather than being the same for everyone.

## Don't Ignore Persistent Abdominal Pain

If abdominal pain keeps returning, lasts longer than expected, or is gradually becoming worse, arrange a medical evaluation.

Avoid repeatedly taking painkillers to mask unexplained pain without understanding the underlying cause. Some medications can also irritate the stomach or affect the kidneys in certain circumstances.

## Final Thoughts

Abdominal pain is a symptom rather than a diagnosis, and its cause cannot always be determined from location alone. Paying attention to when the pain occurs, how it changes, and which other symptoms accompany it can provide useful information for your doctor. If pain is severe, worsening, persistent, or associated with warning signs such as bleeding or fainting, seek medical attention promptly rather than trying to manage it at home.
