## Could Your Sleep Problems Be Sleep Apnea?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Common Sleep Disorders: Symptoms, Causes & Health Effects](/health-library/common-sleep-disorders-symptoms-health-effects/).

Getting enough hours of sleep but still waking up exhausted can be frustrating. If you also snore loudly, wake up gasping, or someone has noticed pauses in your breathing while you sleep, [sleep apnea](/conditions/sleep-apnea/) may be worth investigating.

Sleep apnea is a sleep disorder in which breathing repeatedly becomes reduced or stops during sleep. The most common type is obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), which occurs when the upper airway becomes narrowed or blocked.

Having symptoms does not automatically mean you have sleep apnea. A proper evaluation can help determine what is actually causing your sleep problems.

## Step 1: Recognise the Warning Signs

Possible symptoms of sleep apnea include:

- Loud or frequent snoring

- Gasping, choking, or snorting during sleep

- Breathing pauses noticed by a bed partner

- Excessive daytime sleepiness

- Morning headaches

- Dry mouth after waking

- Difficulty concentrating

- Irritability or mood changes

- Frequent nighttime urination

Not everyone with sleep apnea snores, and symptoms can vary considerably between individuals.

## Pay Attention to Daytime Sleepiness

Repeated breathing disturbances can fragment your sleep even if you do not remember waking up.

As a result, you may feel tired, unfocused, or unusually sleepy during the day. If you regularly struggle to stay awake during work, while watching television, or during routine activities, tell your doctor.

## Step 2: Discuss Your Symptoms With a Doctor

The evaluation usually starts with a medical and sleep history.

Your doctor may ask about your sleep schedule, snoring, nighttime awakenings, daytime sleepiness, medications, alcohol use, existing medical conditions, and other symptoms.

If someone sleeps near you, their observations can be especially useful. They may notice breathing pauses, choking sounds, or unusual movements that you do not remember.

Your doctor may also assess factors such as blood pressure, weight, and other aspects of your general health.

## Step 3: Complete a Sleep Apnea Screening Assessment

Healthcare professionals may use a sleep apnea screening questionnaire to identify people who may be at increased risk.

Screening tools can be helpful, but they do not by themselves diagnose sleep apnea.

If your symptoms and clinical assessment suggest that further testing is appropriate, your doctor may recommend a sleep study.

## Step 4: Have the Appropriate Sleep Study

A sleep study records information about your body while you sleep.

Depending on your circumstances, testing may be performed at home or in a sleep laboratory.

A home sleep apnea test can monitor measurements related to breathing and oxygen levels during sleep. A laboratory polysomnogram can collect more detailed information about sleep stages, breathing, heart activity, oxygen levels, and body movements.

The appropriate test depends on your symptoms, medical history, and clinical assessment.

## What Happens After Testing?

A qualified healthcare professional reviews the results and determines whether the findings are consistent with sleep apnea.

If sleep apnea is diagnosed, treatment depends on the type and severity of the condition and your individual needs. Options may include positive airway pressure therapy, oral appliances, lifestyle measures, positional strategies, or other treatments when appropriate.

## Step 5: Take Daytime Sleepiness Seriously

Untreated sleep apnea can affect alertness, concentration, and daily functioning.

Excessive sleepiness can also create a safety concern, particularly when driving or operating machinery.

If you feel too sleepy to drive safely, do not try to push through it. Stop driving and seek medical advice.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor if you snore loudly with daytime sleepiness, wake gasping, or have high blood pressure that is hard to control. Never drive or operate machinery when you are very drowsy.

## Final Thoughts

If you think you may have sleep apnea, you do not need to diagnose yourself. Start by discussing your symptoms with a doctor. A careful medical history, risk assessment, and appropriate sleep testing can help determine whether repeated breathing disturbances are affecting your sleep. Identifying the problem is the first step toward choosing treatment that can improve sleep quality, daytime alertness, and overall health.
