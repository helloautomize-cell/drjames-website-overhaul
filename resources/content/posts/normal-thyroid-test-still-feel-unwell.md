It can be frustrating to feel tired, gain weight or feel anxious and then hear that your [thyroid](/conditions/thyroid-disorders/) test is normal. A normal result means your thyroid was working within the expected range when tested. It does not mean your symptoms are imaginary. It means the cause may lie elsewhere.

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Thyroid Disorders Explained: Hypothyroidism vs. Hyperthyroidism](/health-library/hypothyroidism-vs-hyperthyroidism-symptoms/).

## What Does a Normal Thyroid Test Mean?

TSH is the main screening test. When it is normal, the thyroid is usually producing the right amount of hormone. Free T4 or T3 may be added in some situations. Doctors interpret results with your symptoms, medicines, pregnancy status and other conditions.

## When Might Retesting Be Reasonable?

- Results were borderline

- Symptoms have changed or worsened

- You recently started or changed thyroid medicine

- You are pregnant or planning pregnancy

- There is a strong clinical suspicion of a thyroid problem

More testing is not always better. Testing should be guided by your doctor.

## What Else Can Cause Thyroid-Like Symptoms?

### Anaemia and iron deficiency

Can cause tiredness, breathlessness and hair loss.

### Vitamin deficiencies

Low vitamin D or B12 can cause fatigue, aches and low mood. Vitamin D deficiency is common in the Gulf region.

### Sleep problems

Poor sleep or sleep apnea leads to daytime tiredness.

### Mental health

Depression and anxiety can cause fatigue, sleep changes and concentration problems.

### Diabetes and prediabetes

High or unstable blood sugar can cause tiredness.

### Medicines and lifestyle

Some medicines, alcohol, caffeine and long working hours affect energy.

### Other conditions

Heart, kidney or liver problems and infections may also be involved.

## What If You Already Take Thyroid Medication?

Do not increase, decrease or stop your dose on your own. Your doctor may review how you take it, such as on an empty stomach and away from calcium or iron supplements, and check other medicines that affect absorption.

## What Should Your Next Steps Be?

Keep a symptom diary, note sleep patterns, diet and stress, and book a review with your doctor. Ask whether tests for anaemia, vitamin levels, blood sugar or other conditions are appropriate.

## When Should You Seek Urgent Help?

:::emergency

Seek urgent care for chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, confusion, a very fast or irregular heartbeat, or thoughts of self-harm.

:::
