## Does Seeing a Nephrologist Mean Something Is Seriously Wrong?

For the wider picture, see our guide on [Kidney Health 101: Warning Signs You Shouldn’t Overlook](/health-library/kidney-health-101-warning-signs-you-shouldnt-overlook/).

Being told that you need to see a nephrologist can naturally feel worrying. But a referral does not automatically mean that you have severe or permanent kidney disease.

A nephrologist is a doctor who specialised in kidney health and kidney-related disorders. Your primary care doctor may recommend a consultation when blood or urine tests show an abnormality, when blood pressure is difficult to control, or when there is a concern that requires more specialised evaluation.

In many cases, the purpose of a referral is simply to understand the problem early and decide what should happen next.

## Why Might Your Doctor Refer You?

There are several reasons a nephrology referral may be appropriate.

These can include:

- Abnormal kidney function blood tests

- Protein or blood detected in the urine

- Persistent changes in kidney function

- Difficult-to-control high blood pressure

- Recurrent [kidney problems](/conditions/kidney-problems/)

- Electrolyte abnormalities

- Suspected or established chronic kidney disease

A referral may also be recommended when the cause of an abnormal kidney test is unclear.

## An Abnormal Test Does Not Always Mean Kidney Disease

Kidney blood tests can be influenced by several factors. For example, creatinine, a commonly measured waste product used to assess kidney function, can vary depending on factors such as muscle mass and other circumstances.

This is why doctors generally interpret kidney test results together with your medical history, medications, urine tests, and previous results rather than relying on one number.

## What Does a Nephrologist Do?

A nephrologist evaluates conditions affecting the kidneys and helps determine how best to protect kidney function.

During an appointment, the specialist may review:

- Your previous blood and urine test results

- Blood pressure measurements

- Medical and family history

- Current medications and supplements

- Other conditions such as diabetes or hypertension

- Changes in kidney function over time

Additional blood or urine tests, imaging, or other investigations may be recommended depending on the situation.

Treatment depends on the underlying problem. It may involve medication adjustments, blood pressure or diabetes management, lifestyle measures, or monitoring over time.

## Why Early Specialist Evaluation Can Be Helpful

Kidney disease can sometimes progress without obvious symptoms. A specialist consultation can help identify potentially important changes before they become more advanced.

A nephrologist may also help determine whether an abnormal result represents a temporary issue, a medication-related problem, or an ongoing kidney condition that requires monitoring.

## Don't Panic About the Referral

A referral is best viewed as an opportunity to get more information, not as a diagnosis of kidney failure.

Bring a list of your medicines and supplements and, if available, previous blood and urine test reports. This can help the nephrologist understand how your kidney health has changed over time.

## When to See a Doctor

:::emergency

Do not delay the appointment. Seek urgent care if you have very little urine, marked swelling, severe weakness, confusion or shortness of breath. Bring your medicine list and earlier test reports to the visit.

:::

## Final Thoughts

A referral to a nephrologist can sound alarming, but it does not necessarily mean that your kidneys are failing or that you have advanced disease. Often, the goal is to understand an abnormal finding, identify its cause, and determine whether monitoring or treatment is needed. If your doctor recommends a nephrology consultation, consider it an important step toward understanding and protecting your kidney health.
