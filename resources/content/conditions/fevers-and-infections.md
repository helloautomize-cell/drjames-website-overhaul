## Who should see a physician about this

- Fever lasting more than two to three days, or repeatedly returning.
- Fever with rash, stiff neck, breathlessness, confusion or severe headache.
- Infections that keep coming back: urine, skin, sinus, dental or chest.
- Fever after recent travel outside Oman.
- Fever in someone with diabetes, low immunity, or in an older adult.

## What happens at your visit

Dr. James maps the timeline: when the fever started, its pattern, what came with it, travel, contacts, and medicines already taken. A full examination follows: throat, ears, chest, abdomen, skin and urine check as appropriate.

Blood or urine tests are done when they will actually change the answer, not as a routine bundle. You leave knowing the most likely cause, whether an antibiotic is genuinely indicated, and exactly which warning signs mean come back now.

## How to prepare

- Note the fever pattern: times of day, highest readings, how it responds to medicine.
- Bring any antibiotics or medicines already taken, including doses.
- List recent travel, contact with ill people, and insect bites.

## After your visit

You get a clear expected course: what should improve and by when, plus the warning signs that need immediate return. If tests were taken, results are explained and the plan adjusted. Recurrent infections get a search for the underlying cause, not just repeated courses of antibiotics.

## When to get help straight away

Call 9999 or go to emergency for fever with confusion, a non-blanching rash (a rash that does not fade when pressed), severe headache with neck stiffness, difficulty breathing, signs of severe dehydration, or a seizure. In babies, older adults and diabetics, err on the side of going.
