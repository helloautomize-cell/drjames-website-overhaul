## Who should see a physician about this

- A blood test showed abnormal TSH, T3 or T4.
- You feel tired, cold, constipated, or are gaining weight without reason.
- Your heart races, your hands shake, or you lose weight without reason.
- You have a swelling in the front of your neck or a thyroid nodule on a scan.
- You take thyroid medicine and want your dose reviewed.
- Thyroid disease runs in your family, or you are planning a pregnancy.

## What happens at your visit

Dr. James asks about your symptoms, medicines, supplements and family history, then examines your neck, pulse, skin and reflexes. He reviews your thyroid function tests (the blood tests TSH, T4 and T3) and may repeat them or add an antibody test to find the cause.

If a lump or nodule is suspected, an ultrasound may be arranged. He then explains in plain words whether the gland is underactive, overactive, or simply needs watching.

## How to prepare

- Bring your previous thyroid reports so trends can be compared.
- Bring your medicine and supplement list. Biotin, a common hair supplement, can disturb thyroid test results, so mention it.
- If you take thyroid tablets already, take them as usual and note the dose.

## After your visit

You leave knowing what your numbers mean and the plan: start, adjust or simply monitor. Repeat tests are scheduled at the right interval, not too early to be useful and not too late to matter. Pregnancy plans are discussed early, because thyroid targets are stricter then.
