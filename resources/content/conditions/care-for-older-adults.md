## Who should see an internist about this

- An older parent manages several conditions and several medicines at once.
- New symptoms like falls, sudden confusion, poor appetite or weight loss.
- Regular review of diabetes, pressure, cholesterol and kidney function together.
- A medicines list has grown long and needs checking for interactions.
- Family wants one doctor who knows the whole story.

## What happens at your visit

Dr. James reviews the full picture: each condition, each medicine and why it was started, recent reports, falls, appetite, sleep, mood and memory concerns. Examination covers pressure lying and standing (a drop on standing causes falls), heart, chest and basic function.

Medicines are reviewed together rather than one by one: what still earns its place, what interacts, what can be simplified. Family members who join leave knowing the plan in plain words.

## How to prepare

- Bring every medicine in a bag, or a complete photo of the boxes.
- Bring recent reports, discharge summaries and any monitoring diary.
- A family member who knows the daily routine is welcome to come along.

## After your visit

The plan is written clearly: which medicines, which targets, which tests and when. Follow-up is scheduled at a sensible interval, and family can reach us on WhatsApp or phone for the small questions that come up between visits.

## When to get help straight away

In older adults, emergencies often look different: sudden confusion, a fall, refusal to eat, or new weakness can signal infection or worse. Go to the nearest emergency department for stroke signs (face droop, arm weakness, speech trouble), chest pain, severe breathlessness or unresponsiveness.
