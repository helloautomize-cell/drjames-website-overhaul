  - q: "How do I know if it is a cold or a chest infection?"

## Who should see a physician about this

- A cough with phlegm, fever or chest discomfort lasting more than a few days.
- Breathlessness, wheeze, or chest pain when breathing.
- You feel seriously unwell, or an older relative suddenly becomes confused with an infection.
- Chest infections keep coming back, or a previous pneumonia needs follow-up.
- You smoke, have diabetes, or have a long-term lung or heart condition and get a chest infection.

## What happens at your visit

Dr. James asks how the illness started and progressed, then checks your temperature, oxygen level, breathing rate and listens to your chest. Many infections can be assessed on examination alone. A chest X-ray is arranged when pneumonia or another cause needs ruling out.

You get an honest answer about whether the illness is viral and just needs support, or bacterial and needs antibiotics, plus clear instructions on what should improve and when.

## How to prepare

- Note when the illness started and how symptoms changed day by day.
- Bring any medicines already taken for it, including leftover antibiotics.
- Bring old chest X-rays or discharge summaries if you have them.

## After your visit

You leave knowing the diagnosis, the treatment if any is needed, and the warning signs to watch. Follow-up is arranged if an X-ray was done or recovery is slow, and older patients or those with other conditions get a closer check.

## When to get help straight away

Call 9999 or go to emergency if you or a relative has severe breathlessness, blue lips, chest pain, coughing blood, confusion, or is too breathless to speak or drink. In older adults, a chest infection can look like sudden confusion or a fall rather than a cough.
