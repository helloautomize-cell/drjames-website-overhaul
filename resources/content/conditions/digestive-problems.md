## Who should see an internist about this

- Acidity or heartburn most days, or needing antacids regularly.
- Bloating, cramps or bowel changes lasting more than a few weeks.
- A new change in bowel habit, especially after 40 or 50.
- Difficulty swallowing, vomiting, weight loss, or blood or black colour in stools.
- Repeated nausea or abdominal discomfort without a clear cause.

## What happens at your visit

Dr. James takes the history that usually makes the diagnosis: which symptoms, their timing, relation to meals, stress, travel, medicines like painkillers, and any alarm features. He examines your abdomen and checks for anaemia clues like pallor.

Tests are chosen for a reason: blood tests for anaemia or inflammation, stool tests for infection or hidden blood, an H. pylori check (a stomach bacterium linked to acidity and ulcers), or a referral for endoscopy when the pattern calls for it.

## How to prepare

- Note your symptom pattern: timing, triggers, what relieves them.
- Bring your medicine list, especially painkillers, iron and supplements.
- Bring older blood or scan reports related to the abdomen.

## After your visit

You leave with the most likely diagnosis in plain words and the plan: food and habit changes, medicines if needed, and which tests, if any, come next. Alarm findings are acted on promptly with referral for endoscopy or specialist review.

## When to get help straight away

Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, vomiting blood or black material, black tarry stools, or a hard swollen abdomen need urgent assessment the same day. Fainting or chest pain with vomiting needs the emergency department.
