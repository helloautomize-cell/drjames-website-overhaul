  - q: "Is my joint pain arthritis?"
    a: "Not always. Osteoarthritis, the wear-and-tear type, usually worsens with use and eases with rest. Inflammatory arthritis like rheumatoid causes morning stiffness over an hour, swelling and warmth. Muscle strain, tendon problems and gout mimic both. The examination separates them."
  - q: "What is gout and how does it feel?"
    a: "Gout is arthritis caused by uric acid crystals in a joint, classically the big toe. Attacks come suddenly, often at night, with a red, hot, exquisitely painful joint. Between attacks the joint feels normal. Blood tests and the pattern confirm it."

  - q: "Will losing weight help my knees?"

## Who should see an internist about this

- Knee, hip, back, hand or shoulder pain lasting more than a few weeks.
- Morning stiffness lasting over half an hour, or joints that swell.
- Sudden severe joint pain, especially a red hot toe or foot.
- Widespread body aches, muscle weakness or persistent fatigue.
- Pain limiting your walking, sleep, work or prayer positions.

## What happens at your visit

Dr. James maps the pain: which joints, what time of day, what triggers and relieves it, stiffness, swelling, and how it started. He examines the joints for warmth, swelling and range, and checks muscle strength and gait.

Blood tests for inflammation, uric acid, vitamin D and related markers are added when the pattern suggests them, and imaging only when it will change management. The aim is the right label first: wear-and-tear, inflammatory, crystal or soft-tissue pain.

## How to prepare

- Note which joints hurt, when it is worst, and how long morning stiffness lasts.
- Bring previous X-rays, scans and blood reports.
- Bring your medicine list, including any painkillers you already use.

## After your visit

You leave knowing what type of joint pain you have and the plan: activity modification, weight strategy, medicines if needed, and physiotherapy where it helps. Inflammatory arthritis is referred for specialist care promptly, because early treatment changes its course.
