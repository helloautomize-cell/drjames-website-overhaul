## Who should see a physician about this

- Loud snoring most nights, especially with witnessed pauses in breathing.
- Waking unrefreshed, with headaches, or falling asleep during the day.
- High blood pressure or diabetes that is hard to control.
- Weight gain with worsening snoring.
- Long-term insomnia, restless legs, or sleep broken by any cause.

## What happens at your visit

Dr. James asks about your sleep pattern, what your partner has noticed, caffeine, alcohol, shift work, and your medicines. He examines your weight, neck, nose and throat, checks your blood pressure, and screens for related problems like thyroid disease.

When apnea is suspected, a sleep study is arranged to measure breathing, oxygen and sleep stages. The result decides the treatment: lifestyle measures alone, a sleep device, or a referral for specialist review.

## How to prepare

- Ask your partner what they notice at night: snoring volume, pauses, gasping.
- Keep a short sleep diary for a week: bedtime, waking, naps, caffeine.
- Bring your medicine list and any previous sleep study reports.

## After your visit

You leave knowing the likely cause and the next step. If a sleep study is done, the results are explained in plain words and the options compared honestly. Weight, alcohol and sleep-habit advice is practical and specific, because those change the outcome most.

## When to get help straight away

Severe daytime sleepiness is a safety risk. If you are dozing while driving or operating machinery, treat it as urgent and book a visit soon rather than waiting. A witnessed collapse, blue lips or gasping with chest pain needs the emergency department.
