## Who should see an internist about this

- Clinic or home readings are repeatedly above 140 over 90.
- You take blood pressure medicine and want the dose or side effects reviewed.
- Your readings swing a lot, or stay high despite treatment.
- You have diabetes, kidney disease, high cholesterol or a family history of early heart disease.
- You want a proper baseline check because you have never measured it.

## What happens at your visit

Dr. James asks about salt, sleep, stress, alcohol, medicines and family history, then measures your pressure properly in both arms after rest. He examines your heart and checks for signs that pressure has already affected the eyes, heart or kidneys.

He may ask for home readings over a week, basic kidney and sugar tests, and an ECG (a heart tracing). The aim is to confirm whether the pressure is truly high, find what is driving it, and set a realistic target.

## How to prepare

- Bring all your medicines and any home blood pressure readings.
- Bring older reports, especially kidney, sugar and cholesterol tests.
- Avoid coffee, energy drinks and smoking for at least an hour before the visit, because they push readings up.

## After your visit

You leave with a clear target and the plan to reach it: food and salt guidance, activity, and medicines if needed. Follow-up visits are scheduled until the pressure is steady at target, then spaced out. Small questions between visits are answered on WhatsApp or by phone.

## When to get help straight away

Blood pressure needs urgent care when it comes with symptoms. Go to the nearest emergency department if you have a severe headache with blurred vision, chest pain, sudden breathlessness, weakness on one side, or confusion. Do not drive yourself in that situation.
