## Who should see a physician about this

- You wheeze, cough or feel chest tightness in episodes.
- A cough has lasted more than three weeks.
- You get breathless on effort, on lying flat, or at night.
- You have been told you have asthma and your control is not good.
- You use a reliever inhaler more than twice a week.

## What happens at your visit

Dr. James asks about the pattern: when symptoms started, what triggers them, night symptoms, smoke and dust exposure, and your medicines. He listens to your chest and may arrange spirometry (a breathing test) or a chest X-ray when needed.

The goal is a clear label for the problem: asthma, allergy-driven cough, post-viral cough, reflux-related cough or something else. Each has a different fix, so the label matters more than a quick prescription.

## How to prepare

- Bring any inhalers or medicines you use, or photos of them.
- Bring old chest X-rays, scans or breathing test reports.
- Note when symptoms are worst: night, morning, exercise, dust, season.

## After your visit

You leave with a written plan: what the problem is, how to use your inhalers correctly, which triggers to reduce, and what to do during a flare. Inhaler technique is checked at follow-up, because small mistakes are common and change the dose that reaches your lungs.

## When to get help straight away

Breathlessness is an emergency when you cannot speak full sentences, your lips look blue, or a reliever inhaler is not helping. Call 9999 or go to the nearest emergency department immediately. Severe attacks can worsen quickly, so do not wait.
